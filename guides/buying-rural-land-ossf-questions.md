# What septic (OSSF) questions should a buyer settle before closing on rural land?

> Ask for the property's OSSF records and a site evaluation before closing; an unpermitted or failing system is a post-purchase cost, not a discovery to make after moving in.

Reviewed **2026-10-19**.

Use this question list with the seller, the permitting authority, and a licensed site evaluator:

1. Is there an existing OSSF on the property? Ask for the permit, the license to operate if one was issued, and any inspection or pumping records.
2. If a system exists, what type is it, when was it installed, and is it sized for the home or use you plan? A system permitted for a smaller dwelling may not cover added bedrooms.
3. If no system exists, has a site evaluation been done for the parcel? Soil, slope, and setbacks decide what can be installed and where; see the [soil research guide](palo-pinto-county-soil-types-septic.md).
4. Where are the water wells, water features, property lines, and easements? Setbacks from them shape the usable disposal area before any design begins.
5. Is any part of the parcel in a floodplain or a lake jurisdiction? Near Possum Kingdom Lake, also review the [BRA jurisdiction guide](possum-kingdom-lake-septic-considerations.md); separate approvals may apply.
6. Who is the permitting authority for this parcel — the county designated representative or another authorized agent? Confirm with that office what a new or replacement system would require; TCEQ's [permit guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfpermits.html) explains the authority's role, and its [owner advice](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfadvice.html) covers owner responsibilities.
7. If an existing system cannot be documented, price the possibility of a full replacement into the purchase decision rather than assuming repair.

This page is a question list for buyers, not a site evaluation, a design, or a price estimate. Only the permitting authority and licensed professionals who evaluate the parcel can say what the property supports.

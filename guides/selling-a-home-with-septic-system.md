# What should a seller have ready when a home has a septic system?

> Gather the system's records before the listing goes live. Buyers and their inspectors will ask for them, and gaps in the file become arguments about price.

Reviewed **2026-11-03**.

Use this checklist to prepare the records and answers a buyer will request:

1. Where is the permit? Find the OSSF permit for the property, and the license to operate if one was issued. If your own file is thin, the permitting authority may hold copies of both.
2. What records show how the system was kept? Assemble pumping receipts, inspection reports, repair invoices, and any maintenance contract paperwork. The [maintenance guide](septic-maintenance-schedule-homeowners.md) lists the records worth keeping over the years.
3. Can you describe the system plainly? Know the system type, its approximate age, and the tank size. A buyer will ask all three, and an answer of unknown invites a lower offer or a longer option period.
4. What does the disclosure require? Texas Property Code section 5.008 requires a seller's disclosure notice for most single-family home sales, and the notice asks whether the property has a septic or on-site sewer facility. A companion form, Information About On-Site Sewer Facility, documents the system type, age, and service history in more detail.
5. What known problems belong on the form? The disclosure covers what you know. A backup, a failing component, or a repair you decided not to make goes on the notice, not in a conversation after closing.
6. What will the buyer's inspection cover? Expect the buyer to order an inspection during the option period. The [inspection checklist](septic-inspection-checklist-texas-real-estate.md) shows what inspectors look at, so you can fix simple access problems, such as lids buried under landscaping, before they arrive.
7. Who answers the questions you cannot? Send system questions to the installer or provider who serviced it, and permit questions to the permitting authority. TCEQ's [owner advice](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfadvice.html) covers the responsibilities that pass to the next owner.

This page is a preparation checklist for sellers, not legal advice and not a substitute for the disclosure form itself. A real estate agent or attorney can confirm what a specific sale requires.

# Why does an aerobic septic system in Texas need a maintenance contract?

> Texas treats an aerobic treatment unit as equipment that needs scheduled professional checks, so the permit carries a standing duty to keep it under a maintenance contract.

Reviewed **2026-11-03**.

Use this question list when you buy, inherit, or renew service on a property with an aerobic treatment unit:

1. Is there a current contract, and who is the provider? Every new aerobic treatment unit in Texas starts under a maintenance contract with a TCEQ-licensed maintenance provider, and many permitting authorities require a contract for as long as the system operates. TCEQ's [maintenance guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfmaintenance.html) also allows some owners to take over the maintenance themselves after that initial contract, where the local authority permits it. Ask which arrangement covers this system, and for a copy of the current contract and the provider's name.
2. When did the obligation start? A new installation begins with an initial two-year maintenance contract, filed with the permit paperwork. If the system is older, ask how the duty has been covered since that first contract ended.
3. How often does the provider inspect? The provider inspects the unit at least once every four months, three times a year, and reports the results to the permitting authority. Ask to see the recent reports before you rely on the system being in good standing.
4. Is the duty recorded against the property? An affidavit recording the maintenance obligation is filed in the county property records. A buyer inherits the duty along with the system, so ask about the contract before closing, the same way you would ask about the permit.
5. What does the owner do between visits? Keep the chlorinator stocked with the tablet type the manufacturer specifies, keep the access lids reachable, and call the provider when an alarm sounds. The [maintenance guide](septic-maintenance-schedule-homeowners.md) covers the records to keep alongside the contract.
6. Does a conventional system carry the same duty? The contract requirement described on this page attaches to aerobic treatment units. The [conventional and aerobic comparison](conventional-vs-aerobic-septic-texas.md) explains how the two system types differ and why the aerobic design needs the closer watch.
7. What happens if the contract lapses? Ask the permitting authority what a lapse means for the permit and what it takes to return to good standing. TCEQ's [owner advice](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfadvice.html) covers owner responsibilities, and its [permit guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfpermits.html) explains the authority's role.

This page is a question list about a maintenance obligation, not the contract itself and not a substitute for the permit conditions on a specific system. The permitting authority and the licensed provider can confirm what a given installation requires.
